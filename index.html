The linear clock — DIY build

A new way to track the progress of time — simplified for Raspberry Pi Pico W, 48 RGB LEDs, battery power, and a Bambu Lab A1 PETG enclosure.

Why a linear clock

Inspired by Daylight Machines, a linear clock replaces digits and hands with a single horizontal bar of 48 RGB LED blocks — two per hour from 00:00 to 24:00. Each 30-minute block illuminates gradually as time passes, giving you both minute-level accuracy and an immediate sense of how much of your day remains.

To make this build as simple, reliable, and affordable as possible (~$35 total), we refined the electronics down to three off-the-shelf modules and four wires, packaged inside a single-piece 250 mm PETG case engineered specifically for the Bambu Lab A1 build plate.

01 / CONTROLLER

Raspberry Pi Pico 2 W (Eco Sleep)

Underclocked to 48 MHz with Wi-Fi OFF and machine.lightsleep() between 15-second updates (~1.8 mA average). Trades sub-second NTP precision for 7–10 days of unplugged runtime.

02 / RGB DIODES + MOSFET

WS2812B 144 LED/m + Night Cutoff

Cutting a Botland 144 LED/m strip to 24 LEDs (167 mm, 1/hr) halves idle quiescent drain, and a 3 PLN IRLZ44N MOSFET disconnects the strip completely during night sleep (23:00–07:00) for 0 mA night drain.

03 / BATTERY + A1 CASE

2× 18650 Pack + Single-Piece PETG

Two 3400–3500 mAh 18650 cells (~25 Wh) inside a 250×56×52 mm PETG shell that prints flat in one piece on a Bambu Lab A1 (256 mm bed) with zero supports — easily hitting ~8 days per charge.

Battery-first wiring plan (~7–10 days)

Each WS2812B diode draws ~0.85 mA even when black. To stretch a 2× 18650 (6800 mAh) pack to a full week, we combine three simple tricks: underclock the Pico 2 W to 48 MHz with Wi-Fi OFF + lightsleep() (~1.8 mA), use 24 LEDs from a 144 LED/m strip (halving idle LED drain to ~20 mA), and put a single IRLZ44N logic-level MOSFET on GP1 to completely disconnect LED ground during night sleep (23:00–07:00 = 0 mA).

24h Power Budget: 16h daytime (~29 mA at 10% Eco brightness) + 8h night sleep (~1.8 mA) = ~20 mA daily average → ~200+ hours (~8.3 days) per charge! (Pressing any rear button wakes the display immediately at night.)

Connection From To Notes
+5V Power Battery Shield / UPS 5V OUT LED Strip +5V & Pico VBUS (Pin 40) Add 470 µF (≥10V) electrolytic capacitor across +5V & GND near the strip.
Ground + Night Cut Battery GND → IRLZ44N (S→D) LED Strip GND (Pico GND direct) Connect Pico GP1 to MOSFET Gate (with 10 kΩ pull-down to GND). Cuts LED drain to 0 mA at night!
LED Data Pico GP0 (Pin 1) LED Strip DIN (input arrow →) Inline 330 Ω resistor protects the first LED pixel.
Rear Buttons Pico GP14, GP15, GP16 3× Tactile Buttons → GND Firmware enables internal PULL_UP + IRQ wake from lightsleep().

Program your own colors & modes

The entire clock runs on a single readable MicroPython file (firmware/main.py). Plug the Pico W into your laptop, open Thonny, edit any RGB tuple or schedule block, and hit Save — the LED bar updates immediately.
